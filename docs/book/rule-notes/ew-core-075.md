## What it checks

A text the program places (a tag, a symbol's data, a cross-reference, a pin's number, a wire number, a cable mark's
text) that lies on another text of the page. One finding per pair of texts, shown on the page; the message quotes both.

## Why

Two texts on each other cannot be read. The program sets each of its texts at the first of its places that is free;
where a page leaves none, the text stays at its place of least harm, and this rule names it.

## Standard

No standard: a project-hygiene rule.

## Example

Place two contacts so close together that the second contact's tag can stand nowhere but on the first contact's
cross-reference.

## How to fix

Drag one of the texts to a free place, or move a symbol so that its texts find room. A text you move stays where you
put it. There is no quick fix.

## Waiving

Waive it only when both texts stay readable as they stand.
