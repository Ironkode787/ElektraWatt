## What it checks

Every cable with an end in a cabinet and an end outside it (in the field, or in another cabinet) needs a cable gland
where it enters the cabinet: the gland the cable's Properties chose, else the first gland of the workshop stock whose
clamping range holds the cable's outer diameter. The rule reports a gland that is not placed, a gland that is not
known (the cable part gives no outer diameter, or no stock gland seals it), a placed gland hole whose size is no longer
its gland's, and a hole made for a cable that is gone.

## Why

A cable that enters a cabinet without its gland, or through a hole of the wrong size, leaves the cabinet open to
dust and water and the cable without strain relief.

## Standard

No standard: a project-hygiene rule.

## Example

Draw a cable from a cabinet's terminal to a motor in the field and do not place its gland on the cabinet's bottom.

## How to fix

Place the gland on the bottom, the top or a side of the cabinet; give the cable part its outer diameter or choose the
gland; take the new gland's size for its hole, or keep a hole without a cable as a hole. There is no quick fix.

## Waiving

Waive it only for a cable that enters through a gland plate or a connector the shop fits itself.
