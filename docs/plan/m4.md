# M4: from the schematic to the built cabinet

**Status:** running since 2026-11-26 · **Release:** 0.4.0

## Summary

**What M4 is.** M4 takes a finished schematic to the workshop. The panel builder:

- places the devices on the plate and in the door, and the program finds each wire's way through the ducts;
- gets every wire with its ends, colour, size, length, the ferrule or lug for each end and the text of its markers;
- prints the labels with the software his shop already has;
- gets, for each strip, what to mount in which order, and for the cabinet what to cut and what to order;
- gets the holes and cut-outs of the plate and the door as a list and as a drawing;
- hands all of it over as one package.

**How it was sorted.** As [M5](m5.md) was: the **easy wins first and whole**, which build on what 0.3.5 has and
which a person feels at once; the **hard battles in their smallest whole form**; the rest to a later milestone,
each with a line why (section 5). The breakdown's M4 was written before the program existed. Read against the
code of 0.3.5, three things changed:

- **Wire lengths do not need 3D.** The 2D layout has the plate, the rails, the ducts and each device's footprint
  and depth, and [12](../design/12-3d-routing-thermal.md) §9 already computes a length from them. Routing and
  lengths are built on the 2D layout. The 3D view adds no data the workshop needs and is not M4's.
- **No crate is added where a home exists** ([04](../design/04-architecture.md) §3.4, §3.6). The breakdown named
  `ew-mfg`, `ew-ewpp`, `ew-panel` and `ew-viewer`: a query over records lives in `ew-derive`, a list's rows in
  `ew-reports`, a writer in `ew-export`.
- **A machine or a label program is served where its file format is published.** The breakdown named five makers'
  formats and cited no specification for any. M4 builds the profile mechanism and one generic profile of each
  kind; a named preset comes with its specification or with a shop that uses the machine.

**Design, then build.** Every work package has a design phase that ends in an accepted design, and then a build
phase (section 2). No code of a work package is written before its design is accepted.

**Size.** Twelve work packages in three steps, and the open rows a person meets at the window (section 6). The
first step alone gives a workshop its lists from the schematic, with no layout drawn.

## 1. What M4 is for

**Purpose.** A panel builder can build a cabinet from what ElektraWatt hands him, without a second list kept in a
spreadsheet. The journey of one job at M4's end:

1. The schematic is done and checked, as today.
2. He places the devices on the plate, as today, and now also in the door: a pushbutton or a lamp sits in a
   cut-out of the door.
3. He lets the program route. It shows each wire's way through the ducts and how full each duct is; he accepts.
   A wire has a length, and the wire list says where the length comes from.
4. He prints the production wire list: per wire the two ends as the fitter finds them, colour, size, length, the
   treatment of each end and the marker's text.
5. He exports the labels: one file per label stock, for the label program his shop has.
6. He prints, per strip, the assembly list, and for the cabinet the cut list of rails, ducts and wires and the
   list of consumables to order.
7. He prints the hole table of the plate and the door, or exports the drawing for drilling.
8. A change after release gives him a rework list: what to pull, what to add, what to mark anew.
9. He exports one package that holds all of it, the same bytes on every machine.

## 2. Design, then build

**The design phase** of a work package ends in these, and is not done before each is there:

1. **The user's side.** Who does this, at which moment of the job, what he sees and what he holds in his hands
   afterwards: the journey in numbered steps, in his words.
2. **Pictures.** Every new or changed view of the window as a mock-up at 1920 × 1080, and every output as the
   workshop gets it: the page or the file, written out by hand for the sample project's cabinet.
3. **The records and their homes.** Each new record, member, query, list kind, command, rule and text, with the
   crate that holds it by [04](../design/04-architecture.md) §3; what the stored format gains; what is declared
   once and handed out. A text is a key with arguments; a setting is a typed member of the project's settings.
4. **What is left out,** each with a line why and where it goes.
5. **The acceptance,** restated on the sample project (section 7).
6. **The design document rewritten** to say all of this in the present tense: it is the specification the build
   follows. Examples of the retired reference project, references to sections that are gone and open questions
   leave the document in the same change.

A design is accepted by the release manager. A design of something the owner asked for by name, or a matter of
taste on a page, is shown to him as pictures first. A design lane writes documents and pictures only.

**The build phase** follows [the orchestration notes](../../.claude/orchestration.md): one brief and one lane per
work package, the accepted design as its specification, additive growth of the format, a review by area, the whole
check at the gate. A lane that finds its design wrong stops at that point and reports; the design is changed
first.

Where two work packages share one design question they are designed together, by one designer, and built apart.

([02c](../design/02c-nonfunctional-requirements.md), NFR-MAINT-11). Each build phase adds to

- **AC-34, restated.** On the sample's cabinet no device overlaps another or the closed door; Route gives every

  of its wires stale until Route runs again.

## 8. Decisions

By the release manager, on the owner's word of 2026-11-25 that the shape of M4's work packages is his to set:

1. Design, then build, for every work package (section 2).
2. Routing and lengths on the 2D layout.
3. No new crate where a home exists.
4. A named machine's or label program's preset only with its published format.
5. WP-S1.28 keeps what the workshop needs; the rest is WP-S1.38 in M6.
6. Thermal stays after 1.0.

By the owner, 2026-11-26:

7. **The 3D view and the STEP reader are not M4's.** 0.4.0 is released after step 3; a 3D viewer is built when a
   user asks to see his cabinet (WP-S4.03 and WP-S4.04 leave the milestone).
8. **The title block and the rulers are changed on the page,** a field of one's own is made from the cell that
   prints it, and columns and rows are set at the frame's numbers with a preview. A cross-reference into a page
   whose rulers are not drawn prints the page alone, also in an imported project.
9. **The wire list prints the fitter's columns by default;** the engineering columns are one tick away. An
   existing project's wire list page prints differently from then on.
10. **A strip's own accessories count in its width** on the rail, as those a part brings do. An existing layout's
    strips grow by them.
11. **The shop the defaults are made for:** a wire's marker names the far end; no ferrule into a spring or push-in
    clamp, a ferrule into a screw clamp; holes and cut-outs are made by hand from a full-size drawing and on a
    machine from a DXF, so step 2 builds the hole table, the printed template and the DXF.
12. **The cabinet's surfaces are a page each** (the door seen from outside); the corner a surface is measured from
    is a setting of the surface, bottom-left by default; tapped holes in the plate are the default.
13. **Cable glands come from the cables, in M4:** a cable that leaves the cabinet brings a gland sized from its outer
    diameter, which waits to be placed on the bottom.
14. **Route previews every wire's way;** a duct warns above 60 % fill, a wire gets 50 mm at each end and 300 mm
    across the door's hinge, as project settings with those defaults.
15. **The shop's label program** imports an Excel file and maps its columns itself: the generic label file serves
    it, and the guide gets a page for that import once the owner has tried it.
16. **An existing tube cut list page prints the cut list's Tubes group:** one row per piece length, each piece
    rounded up to the cut increment, where it printed a total per tube. A page with columns, a sort, a grouping
    or rows set by hand keeps them.
17. **Parts are redesigned from the root** (WP-S1.39): one place to add, change and view a part, the part editor
    and the symbol editor included. A feature works with a part a person makes himself, of every kind: a design
    or a build that reads a datum from a part says where a person enters it in the window and what is said when
    it is missing, and shows it with a part that is not in the starter content.
18. **Parts is a screen of its own,** and a part stands on one sheet with its symbol and its cabinet picture
    beside it. A part a person makes is his and goes to his own parts; a starter part is never changed, he makes
    his own from it.
19. **The shop the place is made for:** a project carries its parts to another PC, where they are kept among
    one's own; no prices on parts; a single part is typed from its data sheet and a family comes from a
    spreadsheet; the workshop stock is the same for every job and is what a new project starts with.
20. **A symbol of one's own is drawn often:** New symbol is one click from a page, opens the drawing at once,
    and saving places the symbol on that page.
21. **An existing project's panel page draws its holes too:** the fixing holes of its rails and ducts, the earth
    stud and the corner the hole table measures from, as a new project's does. Nothing on the page moves. The
    plate's page carries no caption (a plate is always seen from the front); the other faces keep theirs.
22. **The package's dialog has two panes:** what goes in on the left; the check, the revision and personal data
    always in view on the right. The package is a ZIP with folders by kind (lists, labels, drilling), its
    contents and the drawings as one PDF at the top.
23. **A wire without a length stops nothing.** The check for it stays as built and a release is not refused for
    it; the package's dialog and its contents name the wires without a length and say why. A project made
    before this version releases as it did.
24. **No wire file for a machine now.** The shop has no cutting machine and no machine's format has been read;
    the file, its profile and the list for the hand wait for a shop that asks with its format
    ([later](backlog-later.md)). The label file's profile gains its file form and nothing else.
25. **A corrected accessory counts everywhere, the strip on its rail too** (owner, 2026-11-27). Where a project
    holds its own corrected copy of an end plate or a bracket that a terminal's part declares, the lists and the
    checks count the copy and the strip on a panel page is drawn by the copy's width, on existing pages as well.
    A project without such a copy reads as it did.
26. **A list's Excel file is in the project's language; its CSV stays English** (owner, 2026-11-27). People read
    the Excel file, programs read the CSV. It holds for a list's own export, the command line and the package
    alike. The label files keep their headings in both forms: a label program maps them once.
