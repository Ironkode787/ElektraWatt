# M5: fluid power

**Status:** decided, not started · **Base:** (the commit M5 starts from) · **Release:** 0.5.0

## Summary

**What M5 is.** M5 makes ElektraWatt the place where a machine's pneumatics are designed, not only drawn. The
designer:

- makes his maker's parts from their data sheets, in the data sheets' words, or brings them in as EPLAN data
  (Festo first);
- gives each cylinder its sensors, wired or plugged into an input;
- fills a valve terminal of the right size from a table, and draws a large one over as many pages as it needs;
- drives a cylinder from a valve position in one step;
- knows the air his machine consumes and the force each cylinder gives.

Every tube leaves the desk with a number, a size, a length, its fittings and a label.

**How it was sorted.** M5 holds the **easy wins first and whole**: work that builds on what 0.3.0 has and that a
person feels at once. It holds **two hard-fought battles, each in its smallest whole form**, both by the owner's
decision:

- a valve terminal's size chosen when it is placed;
- a large terminal drawn in parts over several pages.

Both ride in one format version (accepted). The rest goes to the backlog, each with a line why:

- the valve and cylinder builder;
- routed tube lengths;
- hydraulics;
- the flow view;
- decoding order codes;
- reading the maker's structured product data.

**Size.** Eight work packages, about **27 person-weeks** (18.5 optimistic, 41 pessimistic), in three steps. The
first step alone gives a designer a complete, checked set of fluid documents.

**What a designer used to the tools fluid designers use today would still miss at the end:** a maker's catalogue
inside the program, a shop basket to bring parts in, a terminal built from its order code, a valve and cylinder
editor, hydraulics, and reports he shapes himself (section 6).

**How a work package is built.** The rules of [m3.md](m3.md) apply: one brief and one lane per work package, the
design document as the specification, additive format growth (here with WP-S2.21's one accepted format version),
tested where it is used, reviewed by area and as a whole.

## 1. What M5 is for

**Purpose.** A fluid designer can do the whole pneumatic side of a machine in ElektraWatt, beside its electrical,
PLC and bus side, with his maker's parts. The documents he hands out are complete:

- the circuit;
- the actuator list;
- the air consumption;
- the valve-terminal configuration;
- the tube list, cut list and labels;
- a parts list with every fitting.

Where one project holds both sides, the program ties every movement to its outputs and inputs and checks across the
disciplines.

### The journey of one job, at M5's end

The job is a part-handling station:

- compressed air in through a shut-off valve and a service unit with soft start;
- a 24-position valve terminal on PROFINET in the cabinet, which also drives the line's two other stations;
- two clamp cylinders with two sensors each;
- a lift cylinder with sensors and flow controls;
- a gripper with one sensor;
- a vacuum ejector with a vacuum switch.

The designer uses Festo parts. **New** marks what M5 adds.

1. **Project and page.** He starts from his company template. **Fluid ▸ Fluid page** gives folio 201
   "Pneumatics". **Air supply** places the supply. In **Properties ▸ Medium** he chooses "Compressed air, 6 bar".
2. **His maker's parts (new).** His shop's parts from last year's jobs are in **My parts** already. Three are new
   on this job, and he makes each with **New pneumatic part…** from its data sheet:
   - **The cylinder.** He picks the tile **Cylinder** and types the type code DSBC-32-100-PPVA-N3. Piston ∅ 32 and
     Stroke 100 fill themselves from the code, and he confirms Cushioning and Position sensing in the sheet's words.
   - **The valve.** He picks **Valve for a terminal**. Valve function "5/2, monostable" is a picture to click; he
     types the standard nominal flow rate and the push-in size of ports 2 and 4.
   - **The sensor.** He picks **Sensor**: maker, part number, type code, PNP, M8.

   Where he has EPLAN data of a part (an EDZ from the EPLAN Data Portal), **File ▸ Import…** takes it as in 0.3.0,
   and the cylinder's piston and stroke are again read from its type code.
3. **The terminal (new: size, table, pages).**
   - **Valve terminal…** offers his terminal part "4 to 32 positions". The bar under the page asks **Positions:**,
     and he types 24.
   - Folio 201 holds about eight cells across its drawing area, so the bar says "24 positions do not fit this page:
     8 do. Enter splits them over 201 and two new fluid pages after it." **Enter** places positions 1 to 8 on 201
     and 9 to 16 and 17 to 24 on new pages 202 and 203. Each part carries the terminal's tag and its positions'
     numbers, and a mark at each cut edge names the page the terminal continues on and is a link to it.
   - In its **Positions** table (the terminal's own tab) he selects rows 1 to 3 and chooses **Fill…** with his 5/2
     valve. Row 4 gets a 5/3, row 5 a 3/2. Rows 6 to 20 take the valves of the line's other stations, and rows 21
     to 24 get **Blanking plate**. Each valve stands in its cell on whichever page holds its position.
   - The order code from the maker's configurator goes into the tab's **Order code** field and prints on the
     configuration list.
4. **Drive the cylinders (new).** He right-clicks cell 1 and chooses **Drive a cylinder…**. The **New actuator**
   panel asks:
   - the cylinder;
   - the speed control: one-way flow controls in both lines;
   - the sensors: both ends;
   - the function: "Clamp 1";
   - the duty: 10 cycles per minute, load 300 N.

   A ghost shows the column above cell 1. **Enter** places it in one undo step. **Next position** repeats for
   "Clamp 2" and "Lift".
5. **The sensors' inputs (new; one feature, two ways of connecting).** The **Actuators** table in the bottom dock
   lists every actuator with its sensors. Each sensor is connected either way, and both ways are there from the
   start:
   - **plugged** into an input of a station: the terminal's input module, an I/O hub, a remote I/O station. He
     selects four sensors and chooses **Plug into inputs of -201K1**: I4.0 to I4.3, no wire drawn;
   - **wired**: he chooses **Draw on an electrical page…** for the other three, which hang on the pointer as a row
     of 3-wire sensor symbols. He wires them through the field terminals to the DI card.

   Each cylinder's Properties then read "Retracted: -201B1 · I4.0 (plugged into -201K1)" or "· I0.3 (101-C4)".
6. **Air and force (new).**
   - The **Actuators** table shows, per cylinder, the theoretical force advancing and retracting at the pressure
     that reaches its valve, and the load as a share of that force. It also shows the air per cycle, cylinder and
     tubes counted, and per minute at its cycle rate.
   - **Air consumption**, a new report and a tab beside the Medium table, sums the consumers per supply. It adds the
     leakage allowance set in Fluid settings and compares the total with the service unit's nominal flow.
   - A load at 85 % of the lift cylinder's force raises a warning; a service unit too small, another.
7. **Tubes for the workshop (new).**
   - The **Tube** tool's bar has the size ("6/4 PU ▾"). He switches to 4 mm for the gripper's tubes.
   - Tubes are numbered per page ("201.1, 201.2, …"), the default for a new project.
   - In Fluid settings he sets a cut allowance and the estimated lengths (in the cabinet 0.5 m, cabinet to machine
     3 m, on the machine 1 m), and types the lengths he knows.
   - **Propose fittings…** puts a push-in fitting on each open tube end at a threaded port, chosen by thread and tube
     size, in one previewed step.
8. **Check.** The Messages show:
   - a sensor whose signal reaches no input (new);
   - tubes without a length, before delivery (new);
   - the load warning (new);
   - the spare positions.

   He fixes them, and the check reads 0 errors.
9. **Lists and out.** **Report…** gives him:
   - the **Actuator list** (new);
   - the **Air consumption** (new);
   - the **Valve-terminal configuration** with its order code;
   - the **Tube list**;
   - the **Tube cut list** with allowance and order lengths (new);
   - the **Tube labels**, with the marker part (new; printed by M4's label engine);
   - the **Parts list** with the maker's numbers, fittings, sensors and tubes in metres.

   The TIA Portal tag table carries the solenoid outputs and sensor inputs under the actuators' function texts. The
   PDF goes to the workshop, the parts list to the buyer, the air consumption to whoever sizes the compressor.

## 4. Festo first: how a person brings in the maker's parts

The repository ships no maker's part data in the starter content. That rule stays. "Festo first" (decision 3) means
three things:

1. The parts-by-kind forms use the words of Festo's data sheets, where those are the trade's words, and read a
   cylinder's sizes from its type code.
2. The EDZ import of 0.3.0 is checked with Festo's parts.
3. A private test makes real Festo parts from their public data sheets; the data stays outside the repository.

The shop basket is not a route (decision 10).

What Festo publishes was found in public sources on 2026-11-19. Its pages often need a login, and some details are
from search summaries; each is marked.

| What Festo publishes | Where | ElektraWatt's route | Sort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Type codes** that carry the main data: a cylinder's series, piston diameter and stroke (DSBC-32-100-PPVA-N3: piston 32, stroke 100, cushioning PPV, sensing A) | Data sheets and catalogue pages (DSBC data sheet, `ftp.festo.com`, read) | WP-S2.22 reads piston and stroke for cylinder families coded "series-piston-stroke-…", in the form and for cylinders an EDZ brings in, and shows them for confirmation | **easy** |
| **Data sheet words.** Cylinders: Piston ∅, Stroke, Mode of operation, Cushioning, Position sensing, Pneumatic connection, Operating pressure, Theoretical force at 6 bar (advancing, retracting). Valves: Valve function (5/2 monostable, 5/3 closed …), Type of reset (mechanical spring, pneumatic spring), Manual override (detenting, non-detenting, covered), Standard nominal flow rate (l/min), Operating voltage | Data sheets (DSBC read; valve words from search summaries of VUVG sheets) | WP-S2.22's forms and WP-S1.37's columns use these words. They are also ISO's and the trade's: "Theoretical force", "Standard nominal flow rate", "Valve function" | **easy** |
| **EPLAN data:** macro packages for the CPX terminals (`.ems` and `.zw5`/`.zw6`, not EDZ) from Festo's CAD site, device data on the EPLAN Data Portal for EPLAN users | `cad.festo.com` (read); EPLAN's partner page (read) | ElektraWatt reads EDZ (M3), not `.ems` or `.zw5`. A person with Data Portal access exports an EDZ; the private test checks that a Festo part's fluid functions map to the right function definitions (the mapping table is unchecked today) | **moderate**; a check, not a build |
| **Valve-terminal order codes** ("ident code") made in Festo's configurator | The maker's configurator; support pages (search) | Stored verbatim (built). The positions are filled from the table (WP-S2.21) | Decoding is a **hard battle**: out (FR-FLU-022, 1.x plugin) |
| **Asset administration shells** for about 20,000 catalogue products, with technical-data and nameplate submodels using ECLASS properties | Festo's digital-twin pages, download after login (search) | None. It is the structured route that would fill a part's data without typing | **hard battle**: a backlog row |
| **CAD models** (STEP and 45 other formats) for more than 33,000 products | Festo's CAD site (search; a PDF brochure) | M4's 3D panel concern, not M5's | — |

- https://ftp.festo.com/Public/PNEUMATIC/SOFTWARE_SERVICE/Documentation/2014/EN/DSBC_EN.PDF (read: type code,
  technical data, theoretical force);
- VUVG data sheets on `ftp.festo.com/public/PNEUMATIC/SOFTWARE_SERVICE/DataSheet/` (search summaries);
- https://www.eplan-software.com/partner/festo/ (read);
- https://www.festo.com/digitaltwin and Festo's "digital twin and virtual commissioning" pages (search summaries);
- https://ftp.festo.com/public/PNEUMATIC/SOFTWARE_SERVICE/CAD_Tools/Festo_PARTdataManager/2024/Info_CAD_EN.pdf
  (search summary);
- https://www.festo.com/us/en/s/air-consumption/ (the calculator; refused the fetch).

**The Festo intake test** (private; the data stays outside the repository, as the private corpus does). The pilot
takes six real parts and their public data sheets: a DSBC cylinder, a valve for a terminal, a cylinder sensor, a
one-way flow control, a push-in fitting and a tube. Then:

1. **Make.** He makes each in **New pneumatic part…** from its data sheet.
2. **Use.** He uses the parts on the sample machine.

The run passes when:
- each form asks only its kind's fields, in the sheet's words;
- the cylinder's piston and stroke come from its type code;
- the parts list shows the six Festo numbers;
- the air consumption of the cylinder matches the maker's public air-consumption calculator for the same piston,
  stroke and pressure, within rounding;
- the theoretical force matches the data sheet's "Theoretical force at 6 bar".

If the pilot has an EDZ of one of the parts (it needs access to the EPLAN Data Portal), its import is checked too:
the part's fluid functions must map to the right function definitions, and a cylinder's sizes must come from its
type code.

#### WP-S1.37 Air consumption and force (easy)

This is its own work package, not part of the actuator one, because it serves another reader: whoever sizes the
compressor and the service unit, and the designer choosing a cylinder. It reads the actuator list's rows and the tube
lengths, and adds data, a report and three rules.

**What a designer wants from it, and what it gives.**

| Wanted | What M5 computes | Honest because |
|---|---|---|
| Air per cylinder, per cycle and per minute | Free air per double stroke = (π/4·D²·s + π/4·(D²−d²)·s) · (p + p_atm)/p_atm (single acting: the advancing half), plus the inner volume of the working tubes (π/4·id²·L, typed or estimated lengths) at the same pressure; × cycles per minute | The geometry, the tube sizes and lengths, and the pressure that reaches the valve (media propagation, a regulator's set pressure included) are all in the model |
| Air per machine, for the compressor and the service unit | Per supply: the sum of the consumers in its zone, plus a leakage allowance (a percentage in Fluid settings), against the nominal flow of its service unit | A sum of honest parts. It is the **average** demand at the stated cycle rates |
| A cylinder's force against its load | Theoretical force advancing p·A and retracting p·(A − a); the load (typed) as a share; a warning above 70 % (the share is a project setting) | The same formula and words as the makers' data sheets ("Theoretical force at 6 bar") |
| Valve and tube flow against the cylinder's speed | The flow a stated speed needs (A·v·(p + p_atm)/p_atm) beside the valve's standard nominal flow; an information when the valve's is lower | Two numbers side by side. No speed is predicted |

**What is left out, because it would need simulation.**

- Cycle and stroke times, and the speed a cylinder actually reaches.
- Pressure drops along tubes and fittings, and the effect of flow controls.
- Peak demand from the timing of movements (only the average is given).
- Cushioning and impact energy.
- Receiver and compressor duty sizing beyond the flow figure.

**Where the numbers are entered.**

- **Part data (with WP-S7.09 and WP-S2.22's forms):** piston and rod diameter, stroke and acting (or read from the
  type code); a valve's standard nominal flow; a service unit's nominal flow.
- **Per actuator,** in its Properties group **Duty** and in the Actuators table: **Cycles per minute**, **Load** (N),
  and **Speed** (mm/s, optional).
- **Per project,** in Fluid settings: the leakage allowance and the warning share.

**Where they are shown.**

- **The Actuators table** gains Force advancing, Force retracting, Load share, Air per cycle and Air per minute.
- **Air consumption**, a new report and a tab beside the Medium table: per supply its consumers, Nl per minute, the
  tubes' share, the leakage, the total, and the service unit's nominal flow.
- **The Medium table** gains a Consumption column.
- **The checks:** EW-FL-018 (load), EW-FL-019 (service unit), EW-FL-020 (valve flow, information).

**User stories.**

- *The designer:* "Before I order the service unit, I want the machine's air at its cycle rates, tubes included, so
  that it is big enough."
- *The customer's plant engineer:* "How much air does this machine take?"
- *The designer:* "When I pick a 32 mm cylinder for a 400 N load, tell me that it is at 83 % of its force at 6 bar."

**Workflow.** In the Actuators table he types the cycles per minute in one cell and fills them down. He types the two
loads he knows. The Air consumption tab shows the total, and a warning names the service unit that is too small.

**Done means.**

- The Festo intake test's cylinder matches the maker's calculator and its data sheet's force.
- The sample machine's Air consumption report matches AC-43.
- A persona run as a designer can tell whether the service unit suffices.

**Design first.**

- **A new doc 11 §5.7 "Air consumption and force":**
  - the formulas;
  - the atmospheric pressure used;
  - which tubes count (the working tubes between the valve and the actuator);
  - single acting, rodless and grippers;
  - the units (Nl, Nl/min);
  - the part fields (`fluid.cylinder`, `qn`; additive);
  - the device fields (additive);
  - the rules.
- **A mock-up** of the Actuators table's new columns and the Air consumption report.

#### WP-S2.23 Valve terminals over several pages (the second hard battle, smallest whole form)

It is its own work package because it is a design of its own: a new kind of drawing, a new command and new checks.
It builds on WP-S2.21's format version and positions table, so it starts after it.

**The person.** He has a terminal of 24 or 32 positions. His fluid page (A3 landscape, the 4 mm grid) holds about
eight cells across its drawing area. A 5/3 valve's cell is wider.

**What 0.3.0 already has, and this package reuses:**

- several boxes of one terminal: a terminal's main function can have more than one drawing as its positions
  (`fluid.draw_terminal`), and `seat_reps` already seats valves on every page that holds such a box;
- the cells and their derived content (`SlotFrame`, `terminal_cells`): each valve in its cell with its output, an
  empty position dashed, a valve drawn elsewhere named by its tag and place as a link;
- the overview generator's page-making: new pages after a given one, with its frame, grid, structure and title;
  break marks derived from the members' pages;
- fluid interruption points and `ip.continue` (one physical tube across a pair);
- cross-reference texts that jump in the window and link in the PDF;
- the Preview tab and one undo step per previewed command;
- the refusal of a terminal wider than the drawing area, which becomes the split's proposal.

**What is new:**

- **A part is a run of positions.** A box drawn as positions gets an optional `positions {first, last}` in its
  `BoxSpec.slots`. A box without it holds every position, as today. Each part is a box of the terminal's main
  function on its page.
- **The drawing of a part:**
  - its cells and their numbers;
  - the sub-base with the galleries 1, 3 and 5 running to its cut edges;
  - the supply end only on the part that holds position 1;
  - at each cut edge a broken edge with a continuation mark;
  - the terminal's tag and "positions 9–16" on every part.
- **The command** `valve_terminal.draw_parts {terminal, parts: [{first, last, page | new_after}]}`. It is previewed
  and one undo step. It makes new pages where asked, places or re-ranges the boxes, and seats the valves.

  Moving a cut moves the valves of the moved positions into their new cells. Their tubes continue through a fluid
  interruption-point pair made in the same step, because their cylinders stay where they are drawn.
- **The window's surfaces:** the proposal on placing, the **Split the drawing** dialog, the Positions table's **Page**
  column and its row commands, the cut cells' menu entries, and the station view's To do lines.
- **EW-FL-021**, and a **Drawn at** column in the valve-terminal configuration list.

**Format.** Nothing beyond the accepted step. `positions` rides in the same new format version as the terminal's size
(WP-S2.21). It cannot be an additive member on its own: an older build would read every part as holding all
positions, and when it edits it would seat valves into the wrong parts. Both members must land in one version before
0.5.0's release.

**User stories.**

- *The designer:* "My 32-position terminal does not fit a page. I want it on four pages without thinking about it,
  and still one terminal with one tag."
- *The designer:* "I decide which positions stand on which page, because I keep one station's valves together."
- *The designer:* "When I add positions later, the drawing grows onto a new page, and nothing on the first pages
  moves."
- *The fitter with the drawing:* "On each page I see which positions these are. At the edge I see where the terminal
  goes on, and the air supply is where it really is: at the left end, once."
- *The PLC programmer:* "Each valve on page 203 still shows the output that drives it."

*The first five minutes: placing a large terminal.*

1. **The proposal.** **Valve terminal…**, his "4 to 32" part, **Positions: 32**. The ghost shows the first part.
   The bar says "32 positions do not fit this page: 8 do. **Enter** splits them over 201 and three new fluid pages
   after it (1–8, 9–16, 17–24, 25–32). **Choose…** sets the parts yourself." Today this terminal is refused.
2. **Enter.** The first part is placed on 201, and the new pages 202, 203 and 204 hold the others at the same place
   on their frames. They are titled "Valve terminal -201K1 (2 of 4)" and so on, in 201's section, and the Preview
   tab lists them. The toast says "Placed -201K1 over 4 pages: positions 1–8 on 201, 9–16 on 202, 17–24 on 203,
   25–32 on 204."
3. **Choose…** opens the **Split the drawing** dialog. It shows a strip of the 32 positions with the cuts marked,
   and a row per part: **Positions** from and to, **Page** ("201 (this page)", an existing fluid page, or "New page
   after …"), and a "fits" or "too wide by 2 cells" note.
   - **Positions per part** fills the rows evenly.
   - Typing a part's first position moves its cut; **Add a part** and **Remove part** change their number.
   - **Apply** is the one previewed command.

*What he sees on each page.*

- **The first part (201).**
  - The supply end with ports 1, 3 and 5, drawn once.
  - Cells 1 to 8 with their numbers, the galleries running to the right edge.
  - At the right edge a broken line and the mark "▸ 202-A3", a link.
  - Over the box, "-201K1" and "positions 1–8".
- **A middle part (202).**
  - At the left edge the mark "◂ 201-H3"; the galleries come in from that edge.
  - Cells 9 to 16; "▸ 203-A3" at the right edge.
  - The tag with "positions 9–16".
- **The last part (204).** A left mark only.
- **Valves in all parts.** A valve stands in its cell on whichever page holds its position, with its tag and the
  output that drives it (`PlcAddress`, derived as today).

*Changing it later.*

- **Moving one position.** At a cut, a cell's menu offers **Move position 8 to page 202** on the last cell of a
  part, and **Take position 9 onto this page** on the first cell of the next. The Positions table's row menu offers
  **Move to the previous part** and **Move to the next part**. Each is the same previewed command. The preview names
  each valve that moves, and each tube that continues through interruption points: "position 8: -201K1-Q8 moves to
  202-B2; its 2 tubes to -201M8 continue through 201/1 and 202/1."
- **Another split.** **Split the drawing…** on the terminal's menu reopens the dialog with the parts as they are.
- **Adding positions.** Raising the count (WP-S2.21) adds the new positions to the last part when they fit there.
  Otherwise the preview offers a new part on a new page after it. Positions never stand in no part without a word:
  EW-FL-021 says so, with the fix **Continue the drawing on a new page**.
- **Joining.** **Join the drawing** (in the dialog, or on the terminal's menu when all parts would fit one page) puts
  the parts back into one box. Each emptied page that held nothing else is offered for deletion in the preview,
  never deleted silently.

*The station view and its To do.*

- **Not drawn yet.** For a terminal drawn on no fluid page, the To do line **Draw on a fluid page…** works as today
  when the positions fit the chosen page. When they do not, it asks which page as today and then shows the split's
  proposal on that page.
- **Some positions undrawn.** The To do line reads "Positions 25–32 are drawn on no page: **Continue the drawing…**".
- **The header.** It names the parts with links: "Drawn on 201 (1–8), 202 (9–16), 203 (17–24), 204 (25–32)".
- **The table.** The Positions table's **Page** column shows each position's page-and-column as a link.

**Across the cut.**

- **The galleries** are derived from the position order, not from the drawing. Air at the supply reaches position 32
  on 204, and EW-FL-010 works unchanged.
- **The checks:**
  - EW-FL-013 (an empty position) points at the cell on its part's page, and a double click goes there;
  - EW-FL-001 (open working ports) points at the valve's own symbol, as today;
  - EW-FL-021 (new) reports positions in no part, two parts holding one position, or a part wider than its page's
    drawing area (a 5/3 can widen a part). Its fixes: **Continue the drawing on a new page**, **Re-split**.
- **The tubes.** A valve's tubes stay drawn on its part's page. Only a moved cut makes interruption points (above).
- **The lists:**
  - the valve-terminal configuration gets a **Drawn at** column (page and column of each position);
  - the table of contents lists the new pages under their titles;
  - the overview pages (Generate overview…) are unaffected: they are their own pages;
  - the tube list is unchanged.

**The smallest whole form,** in M5. For a 32-position terminal to be drawn, read and checked:

- parts with their ranges on chosen pages;
- the proposal on placing and from the station's To do;
- the Split dialog, the cut cells' entries and the table's Page column with its row commands;
- the marks at the cut edges with links both ways, the tag and the positions on every part, the supply once;
- seating per part;
- moving a cut with its valves, the tubes continued through interruption points;
- EW-FL-021 and the Drawn at column.

**What can follow later** (one backlog row):

- dragging a cut on the canvas, or a valve from one part's cell onto another page's;
- carrying a valve's actuator column along with it to the other page, instead of continuing its tubes through
  interruption points;
- intermediate supply plates drawn at their place inside the terminal, and supply ports repeated on each part;
- two parts of one terminal in two rows on one page;
- an automatic re-split when a wider valve pushes a part past its page (until then, EW-FL-021 says so and offers
  Re-split).

**Done means.**

- A persona run as a designer from another ECAD tool gets the job "a 32-position terminal on PROFINET: place it, keep each station's
  valves on one page, fill twenty positions, move one position to the next page, check, export the PDF". He does it.
  In the PDF the marks link both ways, the tag stands on every part, and the supply is drawn once.
- The configuration list's Drawn at column matches the pages.
- EW-FL-021 reports a position left in no part after the count is raised, and its fix mends it.
- On the sample machine's 24-position terminal over three pages, AC-43's lists match.
- An older project with one box per terminal opens and edits unchanged.

**Design first.**

- **Doc 11 §3.2, "A terminal over several pages":**
  - the `positions` member and its rules (consecutive runs, no overlap, every position in one part);
  - the cut edge's drawing and the mark's text (page and column of the other part's first or last cell: "202-A3");
  - which part is the main representation (the one with position 1) and how the others print the tag;
  - the page titles and section of new pages;
  - what moving a cut does to valves, their tubes and interruption points, in which order;
  - EW-FL-021 and its fixes.
- **Doc 05:** the format version shared with WP-S2.21, and the upgrade of a project drawn before.
- **Mock-ups:** the placing bar's proposal, the Split dialog with its strip, a first, a middle and a last part on
  their pages, and the Positions table's Page column.
- **Questions the design must answer:**
  - Does a part start at the same place on each new page, or at the frame's left edge?
  - What does "fits" mean with a 5/3 valve's wider cell, before a valve is chosen?
  - Does Join the drawing delete pages that held only the part, or keep them empty?
  - Where does a part's mark stand when the part's last cell is a wide 5/3?

#### WP-S2.20 Drive a cylinder (easy, smallest form)

**Delivers.** The command and the **New actuator** panel. From a valve, or from a free or held terminal position, it
adds:

- an actuator: cylinder, gripper or swivel unit;
- a speed control: none, or one-way flow controls in both working lines, or exhaust throttles at 3 and 5;
- sensors (WP-S1.35);
- a function text;
- the duty (WP-S1.37).

It lays them out in the valve's column above it: actuators at the top, valves under them, air at the bottom. Tubes
are drawn straight, and the `drives` link and the mounts are made, previewed as a ghost, in one undo step.
**Next position** steps to the next free cell; several cells selected give one column each.

**Out of this form:** "place on the page above" through interruption points when the column does not fit. The panel
then says so in words.

**User stories.**

- *The designer:* "For each valve I say 'a 32/100 cylinder with flow controls and two sensors; it clamps the part',
  and see it drawn as I would have, so that the hundredth movement takes seconds."
- *The designer:* "The page reads as pneumatic drawings read: each movement in its column."

**Workflow and interaction.**

1. **Start.** **Drive a cylinder…** on a valve's or a cell's menu, under the position's heading. On an empty cell it
   reads **Fill and drive a cylinder…**.
2. **The panel.** It opens on the right, not modal. It holds:
   - Actuator (families Cylinders, Grippers, Swivel units; the last used first);
   - Speed (three pictures);
   - Sensors (none, retracted, extended, both, and the part);
   - Extends with (14 by default);
   - Function;
   - Cycles per minute and Load.
3. **Place.** The ghost shows the column. **Enter** places it, and the toast names what was made.
   **Next position** (`Ctrl+Enter`) keeps every choice but the function, which counts on ("Clamp 2").

**Done means.**

- A persona run as a designer from another ECAD tool, with the job "an 8-position terminal driving six cylinders, four with sensors,
  two with flow controls", does it with no tube crossing another or a symbol.
- The actuator list and the parts list are right.
- One undo removes one column.

**Design first.** Doc 11 §7: the column's layout rules (grid spacing, where flow controls and sensors stand, the
14/12 convention, a 5/3), and the refusals in words. A mock-up of the panel with its ghost.

## 9. Decisions (the owner, 2026-11-19)

1. **Hydraulics:** not in M5; after 1.0.
2. **Simulation and the flow view:** both out; after 1.0.
3. **The maker that matters:** Festo. No maker data is shipped. The forms' words, cylinder sizes from type codes, the
   EDZ check and a private intake test serve Festo's published data (section 4).
4. **Air consumption and sizing:** in M5 (WP-S1.37), within what can be computed honestly without simulation.
5. **Realism:** easy wins over hard-fought battles. Every item was sorted (section 3).
6. **Terminal sizes:** the format step is accepted (WP-S2.21).
7. **Tube numbering:** per page, the default, built first.
8. **Sensors:** both ways of connecting, wired and plugged, from the start, as one feature (WP-S1.35).
9. **Valve terminals split over pages:** in M5 and not optional, because large terminals need it (WP-S2.23).
10. **Baskets:** not needed. The shop basket and with it the window's list import leave M5.
